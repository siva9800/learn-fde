## 6.1 An offer is a unit-economics model with your name on it

Everything in sections 02 to 05 is visible in a single offer letter if you know where to look.
The employer has already decided what the role is for and how it will be measured; the offer
encodes it. Four things to read, in this order.

### Equity: how much, worth what, when

At the frontier labs equity is half or more of the total (section 05). At an Indian startup it may
be ESOPs with a four-year vest. Questions the letter must answer or you must ask:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What share of the total is equity, and what price was used to value it? | A 50 lakh "total" that is 30 lakh cash and 20 lakh of options at the last round's price is a 30 lakh job if the company does not grow |
| Vesting schedule and cliff? Refreshers? | Recruiter reports on OpenAI say refreshers can roughly double the cash component at staff level; at a startup there may be none |
| Can it be sold, and when? Secondary sales, buybacks? | Equity you cannot sell is a promise, not pay |
| What happens on leaving, on acquisition, on a down round? | The small print decides whether four years of vesting was worth anything |

A rule for the Indian market in 2026: value a startup's ESOPs at zero when comparing cash, then ask
yourself whether you believe in the company enough to treat them as the upside. That is not
cynicism; it is what the equity is.

### Travel share

Recruiter descriptions of OpenAI's role put travel at up to 50 percent. Ramp's FDEs work remotely
with five to six customers each. Kaagaz AI's model has the FDE in Hyderabad and Vijayawada for
twelve weeks. All three are FDE roles; the travel share says what kind. Ask: what share of weeks
on site, where, for how long per engagement, and who pays for it. An offer that is silent on
travel is an offer that has not decided.

### Customer count per FDE

This is the number that tells you which economics you are joining. Ramp says five to six at once,
remotely, which forces product-shaped work. A frontier lab says one or two deep engagements. A
services firm says "whatever the account needs", which means one, for as long as they bill. Ask:
how many customers does an FDE carry, how long does an engagement last, and what is the
self-sufficiency bar that ends it (Atlassian: about twelve weeks).

### What is measured

Ask directly: "What number will my manager look at in my six-month review?" The answers map
straight onto section 04:

| Answer | What you are joining |
|---|---|
| Renewal, expansion, ARR per FDE, patterns shipped to product | An FDE team with the economics intact |
| Deployment delivered on time, customer satisfaction | A deployment or solutions team; fine, priced as one |
| Utilisation, billable hours, margin on the account | Consulting |
| "We have not decided yet" | A new team; ask who decides, and whether the commercial side or the product side owns it (OpenAI's leader credits product and research owning strategy for keeping services revenue in check) |

## 6.2 The questions to ask a hiring manager

Ask these in the hiring-manager round. They are not aggressive; a manager running a real FDE team
will enjoy them, and a manager running a staffing bench will struggle with them, which is the point.

1. **"How do you measure the FDE team?"** Listen for a ratio or a recurring-revenue figure. "Customer
   happiness" alone is a warning.
2. **"How many customers does an FDE carry, and how long is an engagement?"** Five to six remote,
   one or two deep, or "depends on the account".
3. **"What did the last engagement send back to the product team?"** A named feature or pattern is
   the right answer. Silence means the trap is already forming.
4. **"Tell me about an engagement you turned down."** A healthy team has one. OpenAI's does. If the
   manager has never declined work, the team takes everything, and everything includes services.
5. **"Is the deployment fee fixed or billed by time?"** Fixed means the company wants engagements
   to end. Time-based means the company is paid for slips.
6. **"What is the self-sufficiency bar, and who decides the FDE leaves?"** A date and a test, or
   "when the customer is comfortable", which is never.
7. **"What does the FDE own after go-live?"** The outcome, the renewal, the patterns; or nothing,
   because someone else does.
8. **"Who is the sponsor inside your company for the FDE function: product, sales or services?"**
   The answer predicts whether the team will be measured on revenue that recurs or revenue that
   arrives this quarter.
9. **"What share of the role is equity, at what valuation, and when can I sell it?"** Section 6.1.
10. **"What happened to the last FDE who left?"** Whether the customer kept running is the whole
    model tested once.

Write the answers down. On [Day 46](../../08-capstone-and-career/46-capstone-defence-and-career/README.md)
you compare offers with these ten questions as the rows.

## 6.3 Reading the same offer from Kaagaz AI's side

Flip the table. When Kaagaz AI's CEO asks whether Godavari is worth an FDE (section 08), he is
asking the same four questions about the engagement that you ask about an offer:

| Your question about an offer | The CEO's question about Godavari |
|---|---|
| What share is equity, worth what? | What share of the contract is recurring, and will it renew? |
| Travel share? | How many weeks on site, fixed fee or by time? |
| Customers per FDE? | Does this engagement leave the FDE free for the next lender by week 13? |
| What is measured? | Revenue over FDE cost; ARR per head; the pattern the next lender buys |

An engineer who can answer the right column for her own engagement can read the left column in any
offer, which is why the project comes before Day 46.

## 6.4 Key takeaways

- Read equity (share, valuation, vest, sale), travel share, customer count per FDE and what is
  measured, in that order; each one maps to a business model.
- Ten questions for a hiring manager; the two most revealing are "what did the last engagement
  send back to product" and "tell me about one you turned down".
- The CEO's question about an engagement is your question about an offer with the columns swapped.
