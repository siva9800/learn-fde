## 8.1 The brief

It is the Friday of Day 3. On Day 1 the CEO forwarded the COO's one-line introduction and you wrote
the note on what a forward deployed engagement would look like. On Day 2 you drafted the first two
weeks as a calendar. Now the CEO asks the question a 40-person company has to ask before it commits
one of its four FDEs for twelve weeks: **is Godavari worth an FDE?** He wants two things by the end
of the day, and both go into the engagement folder:

1. **A unit-economics model**: Kaagaz's cost of the FDE, the contract value, the recurring revenue
   if it renews and if two more lenders buy the same pattern, the break-even, and the measures the
   published teams use (revenue over FDE cost; ARR per FDE head), against the services alternative.
   A small standard-library Python script with a printed table, so that he can change a number and
   rerun it in the meeting.
2. **A one-page "is this FDE-shaped?" assessment** using OpenAI's qualifying questions, ending with
   the first "we will not build this" line of the engagement.

All numbers are invented. That is the point of a model at this stage: the arithmetic is simple, and
the value is in which numbers you chose to put in it and which scenarios you chose to run.

**Files:**

| Path | What |
|---|---|
| [`code/unit_economics.py`](code/unit_economics.py) | the model: four scenarios plus two stress tests over 36 months; prints the table and writes `data/godavari-unit-economics.csv` |
| `data/godavari-unit-economics.csv` | one row per scenario per month (216 rows), regenerated by the script |
| [`worksheet/godavari-fde-shaped-assessment.md`](worksheet/godavari-fde-shaped-assessment.md) | the one-page assessment (the answer to part B) |
| [`worksheet/ai-draft-fde-shaped-assessment.md`](worksheet/ai-draft-fde-shaped-assessment.md) | an agent's draft of the same page with at least seven problems, for exercise 3.1 |

Run on **Python 3.14.3** (October 2026), standard library only, from the chapter folder:

```bash
python -X utf8 code/unit_economics.py
```

It finishes in well under a second. `-X utf8` keeps Windows consoles happy; it is harmless elsewhere.

## 8.2 Part A: the model

### The numbers you choose

The script's top section is the whole argument in constants. Each one is a decision the CEO can
argue with, and the comments say why it is there:

| Constant | Value (invented) | Why this number |
|---|---|---|
| `FDE_LOADED_COST_PER_YEAR` | 60 lakh | A senior FDE's CTC in Hyderabad in the 2026 posting ranges, plus travel, equipment, insurance and a share of management. Loaded, not salary: the denominator must carry everything the engineer costs |
| `SERVING_COST_SHARE` | 22 percent of subscription | Inference, the model gateway, support engineering. Hosting is in Godavari's own AWS account, so it is not here |
| `PRE_CONTRACT_INVESTMENT` | 15 lakh | Eight weeks of discovery (Days 4 to 10) before any invoice, plus sales cost. The thing break-even has to recover |
| `SUBSCRIPTION_PER_YEAR` | 1.2 crore | The platform, per lender, per year. The recurring line |
| `DEPLOYMENT_FEE_FIRST` | 36 lakh, fixed | Twelve weeks on site. Fixed, so a slip costs Kaagaz (section 02.2) |
| `DEPLOYMENT_FEE_REPEAT` | 20 lakh | Six weeks for a lender buying the proven pattern |
| `FDE_SHARE_AFTER_DEPLOY_Y1`, `FDE_SHARE_STEADY` | 20 percent, then 10 percent | The engineer's time after handoff. The single most important assumption in the model, and the one the stress tests attack |
| `CUSTOM_BUILD_PRICE` and friends | 1.8 crore fixed, three engineers for nine months, then a 25 lakh retainer at half an engineer; repeats at three quarters | The services alternative, priced the way an integrator would price it |
| `RAMP_RATIO_FLOOR` | 3.0x | Kaagaz's own bar. Not published by anyone |

### The scenarios

Four base scenarios and two stress tests, each a list of 36 `Month` records with revenue, serving
cost, FDE cost, FDE full-time-equivalent and ARR live that month:

| Scenario | What it models |
|---|---|
| A | Godavari signs, deploys, and does not renew: one year only |
| B | Godavari renews for three years |
| C | B, plus a second lender from month 12 and a third from month 18, each on the six-week repeat deployment |
| D | The services alternative: a custom build for Godavari, then two more custom builds for the other lenders, each from zero |
| B1 | B, but Kaagaz said yes to the branch CRM: the FDE stays full-time for all 36 months |
| B2 | The twelve weeks become thirty, the fee stays fixed, and Godavari does not renew |

The measures, one method each: `ramp_ratio` (36-month revenue divided by 36-month FDE cost),
`arr_per_fde_head` (ARR live in month 36 divided by the FDE heads needed to keep it live),
`break_even_month` (the first month cumulative contribution covers the pre-contract investment),
and `peak_fte` (the most FDE capacity any month needs, against Kaagaz's four FDEs).

### The run

```text
Assumptions (all invented): one FDE costs 0.60 cr a year fully loaded; subscription 1.20 cr a year per lender; first deployment fee 0.36 cr; serving cost 22% of subscription; pre-contract investment 0.15 cr.

== Is Godavari worth an FDE? (36 months, rupees, invented) ==
scenario                                        revenue FDE cost contrib.  ARR m36  ARR/FDE rev/FDE peak FTE break-even
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A  Godavari, FDE model, no renewal               1.56 cr   0.24 cr   1.06 cr   0.00 cr   0.00 cr    6.5x     1.00    month 2
B  Godavari renews for three years               3.96 cr   0.36 cr   2.81 cr   1.20 cr  12.00 cr   11.0x     1.00    month 2
C  B plus two lenders buy the pattern            8.56 cr   0.77 cr   6.07 cr   3.60 cr  12.00 cr   11.1x     1.30    month 2
D  Services: custom build, then two more         5.44 cr   4.50 cr   0.94 cr   0.62 cr   0.50 cr    1.2x     5.00    month 3

How to read the columns:
  rev/FDE     revenue divided by FDE cost over 36 months (Ramp's ratio); Kaagaz's own floor is 3.0x

== What turns B into services (36 months, rupees, invented) ==

  the slip, and no renewal         rev/FDE 3.9x  still above the floor
```

### Reading the table in the meeting

Five things to say to the CEO, in order:

1. **Even without a renewal, the engagement pays.** Scenario A clears the floor at 6.5x, because
   the fixed deployment fee covers the twelve weeks and the first year's subscription is margin.
   Break-even is month two for the same reason: the fee pays for the engineer as she works. This is
   why break-even is the least interesting column at this pricing (section 9.3 says so too).
2. **The renewal is the engagement.** Years two and three of scenario B are worth 1.75 crore of
   contribution on a tenth of an FDE. ARR per head goes to 12 crore because the denominator has
   almost vanished. Everything in Parts 2 to 5 of this course (the right problem, the evals, the
   handoff, adoption) is in service of that one row.
3. **The pattern is where the ratio stops depending on luck.** Scenario C adds two lenders at six
   weeks each; the ratio barely moves (11.1x) because repeat deployments are cheap, and ARR at
   month 36 triples. Peak FDE load is 1.3 of Kaagaz's four. This is the row that justifies the
   10 percent of the week spent writing patterns up for the product team (Day 2).
4. **The services alternative earns more revenue and is a worse business.** Scenario D books 5.44
   crore, the most of any row, with 1.2x revenue over engineer cost, five engineers at peak, and
   0.5 crore of ARR per head. It is the left column of section 2.1 with numbers on it: more
   invoices, no compounding, and a revenue line that stops when the engineers do.
5. **The thing that breaks it is time, not price.** B1 is the branch CRM: same contract, same
   subscription, but the FDE never leaves. Revenue over FDE cost falls to 2.2x, below the floor,
   and ARR per head to 1.2 crore. The slip in B2 hurts less (3.9x) because the fee is fixed and
   the engineer eventually leaves. Exercise 2.1 shows that halving the price hurts less than B1
   too. The model's one-sentence conclusion: **the engagement is FDE-shaped as long as the FDE's
   time falls, and the only way it stops falling is a yes to custom work.**

The model is deliberately small (about 220 lines, two dataclasses, no libraries). Read it once; the
point is that the CEO's question has a shape, and the shape is the ratio in section 02 with
Kaagaz's numbers in it.

## 8.3 Part B: the one-page assessment

The model says the economics work if certain things are true. The assessment says whether they are
likely to be true, using the five qualifying questions this course reads out of OpenAI's published
practice (the 2025 interview and the 2026 panel in section 12): FDE-shaped; a realistic path to
recurring revenue; a product hypothesis or a research-interesting problem; value large enough; and
would we take it if it were pure services. The written answer is
[`worksheet/godavari-fde-shaped-assessment.md`](worksheet/godavari-fde-shaped-assessment.md). Its
shape:

| Question | Godavari's answer | The honest part |
|---|---|---|
| FDE-shaped? | Yes | The gap is between "reads a salary slip" and 40 analysts, a 2009 SOAP core, an Excel tracker and an RBI auditor (Day 0). Nobody closes it from a laptop in Bengaluru |
| Path to recurring revenue? | Yes, with a condition | Only if the FDE's time falls below a tenth by month four and the handoff bar is met (Day 28). Otherwise the subscription is a retainer |
| Product hypothesis or research-interesting? | Product: yes. Research: no | Every NBFC needs the same four patterns; two more are in the pipeline. Telugu-English forms are a data problem, not research. The engagement is taken on the hypothesis alone |
| Value large enough? | Probably | 40 analysts, 4.5 hours a file, 31 percent send-backs: enough that 1.2 crore a year is an easy decision if Day 9's baseline confirms it. An assumption until then |
| Take it as pure services? | No, and we said so | The branch CRM, declined |

Four yeses or probablies and one honest no is a good assessment. An assessment with five yeses was
written from the sponsor's pitch (section 9.3). The one most engagements fail honestly is
"research-interesting", and failing it is fine: OpenAI's own practice takes product-hypothesis
engagements without a research angle.

The page ends with a **week-13 test** the CEO can hold the engagement to: FDE time at or below 20
percent and falling; three named patterns in the product backlog; a second NBFC that has seen the
walking skeleton. If none is true by week 13, the engagement is in scenario B1 whatever the invoice
says.

## 8.4 Part C: the first "we will not build this" line

In the second call, the COO asked whether Kaagaz could also give branch managers a screen to track
customer follow-ups: calls made, documents promised, visits. A branch CRM. It is a reasonable
request from his side, and it is the first yes of the services trap from ours.

The test from section 3.5: would a second, named customer want this, in this shape, within a year?
No. No other lender wants Godavari's CRM; a CRM vendor or Godavari's own IT does it better; and it
would keep an FDE on site for a year leaving nothing for the next customer. The model puts a
number on it: B1.

The words, as the assessment records them and as you will say them on Day 10 when the request
comes back:

> "We will not build the branch CRM. It is not our product, no other lender would buy ours, and it
> would keep an engineer here for a year doing work your own IT team or a CRM vendor does better.
> What we will do is expose each loan file's status and its missing-document list through an API,
> so your CRM, or the tracker, can read it. That part every lender needs, and it will be in the
> product."

Notice the shape: the refusal, three reasons in the order the sponsor will accept them, and the
thing you will do instead, which is product work. [Day 8](../../02-discovery-and-decomposition/08-scoping-the-first-90-days/README.md)
owns the full "will not do" list and [Day 32](../../05-working-with-the-customer/32-scope-pushback-and-governance/README.md)
owns the harder refusals. This is the first line of that list, written before the contract.

## 8.5 Build it with AI

An FDE in 2026 would use a coding agent for the script and a chat agent as the sceptical CFO. The
first is routine; the second is where the value is, and where the agent fails in ways that look
like competence.

**Context to provide:** Day 0's facts about Godavari (1,200 staff, 300 branches, 40 analysts, the
2009 SOAP core, the tracker, the RBI audit), Kaagaz's size (40 people, four FDEs), the pricing
shape (subscription plus a fixed deployment fee), the three published measures with their sources
and dates, and the rule that every rupee figure is invented and must be labelled so.

**A strong prompt for the model:**

> Write a standard-library Python 3.14 script that models a 36-month engagement for a 40-person AI
> vendor deploying its platform into one lender. Constants at the top, every one with a comment
> saying why it is that number. Scenarios: no renewal; renewal for three years; renewal plus two
> more lenders at a shorter repeat deployment; a fixed-price custom-build alternative with a
> maintenance retainer; and two stress tests in which the FDE's time after deployment does not
> fall. For each, print revenue, FDE cost, contribution, ARR live at month 36, ARR per FDE head,
> revenue divided by FDE cost, peak FDE FTE, and break-even month against a pre-contract
> investment. Count ARR only for revenue that would still bill if the engineer left. Label the
> ratio floor as the vendor's own choice, not a published figure. Write a CSV with one row per
> scenario per month. No libraries.

**A second prompt (the sceptical CFO, for the assessment):**

> Here is my one-page assessment of whether a customer engagement is FDE-shaped, and the model's
> output. Do not rewrite it. Argue against it as a CFO who has seen three services practices
> dressed as product teams: (1) which answer is taken from the sponsor's pitch rather than from
> evidence; (2) which number in the model is doing all the work, and what happens if it is wrong by
> half; (3) which figure is presented as a fact that is actually an assumption or a recruiter's
> estimate; (4) what the customer will ask for in month four that the "will not build" line does
> not cover. Cite the line of the assessment for each point or say it is a guess.

**What the agent typically gets right:** the script's structure (dataclasses, scenarios, a table,
a CSV); the vocabulary of ARR, contribution and break-even; the five questions once given them; a
plausible-sounding memo.

**What it typically gets wrong for this concept (check each):**

| Mistake | How it shows | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Counts one-time revenue as ARR** | "ARR of 1.56 crore in year one" (the AI draft: subscription plus deployment fee) | ARR is revenue that would still bill if the engineer left; the deployment fee is not, the custom-build price is not, a retainer for a side-build is not (07.3) |
| **Attributes the floor to a published team** | "Ramp requires at least 3x" | Ramp publishes the measure; the floor is Kaagaz's; the memo says so in the same sentence (02.5) |
| **Stress tests that do not bite** | FDE time "rises to 30 percent" and every scenario still passes; the model cannot say no | the stress case is the real request (the branch CRM: full-time, 36 months); if "yes to everything" clears the floor, the floor or the assumptions are wrong (09.3) |
| **Answers the qualifying questions from the pitch** | five yeses; "research-interesting: yes, Telugu documents are novel" | each answer cites a Day 0 to Day 2 fact or is marked an assumption; "research-interesting" is usually an honest no |
| **A "will not build" line nobody asked for** | "we will not build a mobile app" | the line must refuse something the sponsor actually wants (the CRM from the second call), with the second-customer test as the reason (03.5) |
| **Pay figures as promises** | "an FDE on this engagement can expect 40 lakh", from a 28-submission average and a posting range | pay figures carry a date and a source type and never appear in an engagement memo as a promise (05.1) |
| **Default arguments bound to constants** | `def add(..., share_steady=FDE_SHARE_STEADY)`: editing the constant at the top and rerunning changes nothing, silently | resolve overrides inside the function (`None` default, then fall back to the module constant); the chapter's script had exactly this bug on its first draft, and the exercise that asks you to edit a constant caught it |
| **Confuses a headcount with a ratio** | "OpenAI has 90 FDEs, so Kaagaz should hire four more" | the published teams are small relative to revenue; the number that justifies a hire is ARR per head, not a competitor's headcount (07.2) |
| **Agrees with the memo** | asked to review, it praises the structure and suggests a chart | give it the CFO role and the four questions; ask it to argue the opposite conclusion |

The two to watch most are the first and the fourth: an ARR figure that includes one-time revenue
and a set of qualifying answers taken from the sponsor's enthusiasm. Both make a services
engagement look FDE-shaped, which is the exact mistake this chapter exists to prevent.

## 8.6 How do you know it's right?

Whether a human or an agent wrote the model and the memo, the checks are the same:

1. **Reconcile one scenario by hand.** Scenario B: subscription 1.2 crore times three is 3.6, plus
   the 36 lakh fee, is 3.96 crore. FDE cost: three months full-time (15 lakh), nine months at 20
   percent (9 lakh), 24 months at 10 percent (12 lakh), is 36 lakh. 3.96 over 0.36 is 11.0x. If the
   script and your hand disagree, the script is wrong until shown otherwise.
2. **ARR passes the resignation test.** For each scenario, ask: if the FDE resigned in month 36,
   which revenue would still bill next month? That is the ARR column and nothing else. Scenario D's
   0.62 crore is the maintenance retainers; the build revenue is not there. Scenario A's zero is
   correct: the contract ended.
3. **The stress tests bite.** At least one must fall below the floor. If none does, the model is
   decoration.
4. **Every constant has a reason.** Read the comments at the top aloud. "60 lakh because that is
   what a senior FDE costs loaded" is a reason; "60 lakh" alone is not.
5. **The floor is labelled as Kaagaz's.** Search the memo and the script for "Ramp" and check that
   no sentence attributes a number to them that they did not publish.
6. **The qualifying answers trace to evidence.** Each cites a Day 0 to Day 2 fact or says
   "assumption until Day 9". The research-interesting answer is honest.
7. **The "will not build" line is a real request** with the second-customer test as the reason and
   a product-shaped alternative beside it.
8. **No pay figure appears in the engagement artefacts.** The memo is about the customer's
   economics; your salary is section 05's business, dated and sourced, and never a promise.
9. **Editing a constant changes the output.** Change `FDE_SHARE_STEADY` to 0.5 and rerun; B's ratio
   must fall. If it does not, defaults are bound at definition time (8.5's seventh row).
10. **The CSV reconciles with the table.** Sum the `revenue` column for scenario B across its 36
    rows; it is 3.96 crore to rounding.

**Review checklist:**

- [ ] scenario B reconciled by hand; ratio 11.0x
- [ ] ARR column passes the resignation test in every scenario; one-time revenue excluded
- [ ] at least one stress test below the floor; the stress case is the real request (the CRM)
- [ ] every constant has a one-line reason; all labelled invented
- [ ] the 3x floor labelled as Kaagaz's own; no number attributed to Ramp, Dataland or OpenAI that they did not publish
- [ ] five qualifying answers, each with evidence or "assumption"; research-interesting answered honestly
- [ ] the "will not build" line refuses something the sponsor asked for, with the second-customer test and a product-shaped alternative
- [ ] the week-13 test is written and measurable
- [ ] no pay figure in any engagement artefact
- [ ] editing a top-of-file constant changes the output; the CSV sums match the table
