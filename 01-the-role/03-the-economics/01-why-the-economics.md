## 1.1 Two engineers, one title

Two engineers in Hyderabad hold the title "Forward Deployed Engineer" in October 2026.

The first works for a 40-person AI vendor. She spends twelve weeks inside a lender, makes the vendor's
platform read the lender's loan files and talk to its 2009 core system, hands it over, and sends two
things back to her own company: a renewal that will bill every year without her, and three patterns
the product team ships to the next five lenders. Her employer measures her by the recurring revenue
her engagements leave behind.

The second works for an IT services firm. He is billed to a client against a statement of work. He
builds a custom document pipeline for one bank, from zero, with a team of four. When it is done, the
client owns it, the firm invoices the hours, and the next client starts from zero again. His employer
measures him by utilisation: the share of his hours that can be billed.

Same title. Same skills, almost. The pay for the first role in India, in postings this year, runs from
about 22 lakh at a global company's India centre to 70 to 90 lakh for a founding role. The pay for
the second runs from 5 to 10 lakh. That gap is not noise. It is the economics of the two business
models showing up in a salary band, and this chapter teaches you to see it.

## 1.2 Why it matters for your career

Three reasons, all from the research behind this course (the Part 1 plan; sources in section 12):

- **Interviewers ask it early.** "How is this not consulting?" and "why FDE and not a solutions
  role?" come in the first ten minutes of hiring-manager screens at the vendors that run FDE teams.
  The candidates who answer with the economics (recurring revenue per engineer, patterns that become
  product, engagements declined because they are pure services) sound like people who have thought
  about what the job is for. The ones who answer "I like working with customers" sound like solutions
  engineers.
- **You will be offered services work with the FDE title.** Indian postings for the title rose
  nearly 19 times between June 2025 and June 2026 (Indeed data, reported October 2026). About a
  third of the live market sits under adjacent titles, and a share of the postings under the title
  itself are staff-augmentation roles billed against a client's statement of work. The skill of this
  chapter is reading a posting or an offer and telling which one you are looking at.
- **The pay band is the widest in engineering.** Postings in India in July 2026 ran from a 5 to 10
  lakh services tier to 70 to 90 lakh for a founding role: a spread the source calls 18 times. In
  the US, 2026 postings put mid-level total compensation at 300 to 450 thousand dollars, with equity
  half or more of it. You cannot negotiate against a band that wide without knowing what moves a
  role from the bottom of it to the top.

> **Analogy (Telugu audience).** Biryani. The caterer who invents a new recipe for every wedding is
> the consultant: paid for the day, starts from zero the next function, never gets faster. The
> restaurant that perfected one recipe and sends a chef to run a counter at the wedding is the FDE
> model: the chef adapts the dish to the hall and the family, the recipe comes home improved, and
> the restaurant sells it every day whether or not there is a wedding. The chef is paid differently
> from the caterer's cook, because the chef's work makes the restaurant worth more.

## 1.4 The hard parts, named up front

1. **The numbers are soft.** Every pay figure here comes from a recruiter, a career site or a vendor
   blog, not an official survey. Indeed itself said its India FDE sample was too small for a reliable
   median. This chapter dates every figure and names its source type, and you should treat all of
   them as a snapshot that will be wrong by this time next year. Never repeat a band from this chapter
   as a promise, to yourself or to anyone else.
2. **The services trap feels like success while you are in it.** A customer who pays for custom work
   is a happy customer. Revenue arrives. The FDE is praised. The damage (nothing generalised, the next
   customer starts from zero, the product team learned nothing) shows up a year later in the ratio.
   Section 03 gives you the early signs so you do not need the year.
3. **You will have to say no to a sponsor who is offering money.** The first "we will not build
   this" line in the Godavari engagement is written in this chapter, before the contract is signed.
   It is uncomfortable. Section 08 shows the words.
