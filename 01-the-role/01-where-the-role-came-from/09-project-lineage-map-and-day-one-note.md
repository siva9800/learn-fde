## 9.1 The brief

It is the morning the CEO forwarded the COO's one line. Nobody has replied. Before anyone does, you owe
the engagement folder (which does not exist yet; you are creating it) three things and yourself a
fourth:

1. a **one-page lineage map**: which published model Kaagaz AI is following, who plays Delta, Echo and
   Dev here, what we copy and what we do not, with every figure dated and its source type marked;
2. a **timeline** of the role's spread, as data, rendered and checked by a trivial script so that no
   row is undated or unsourced;
3. the **day-one note**: a forward deployed engagement at Godavari versus a services proposal, one
   page, for the CEO and the head of sales, with no model names in it. This is the Part 1 plan's
   "first artifact in the engagement folder";
4. a **reflective exercise**: the Godavari engagement written as a Delta around 2015 would have
   described it to a Dev, then translated line by line into Kaagaz in 2026.

Code is almost nothing in this chapter on purpose. The thinking is in the three pages; the script
exists so that the timeline's discipline (date, source, type, every row) is enforced by something
other than your memory.

## 9.2 Part A: the lineage map

Write it before reading the chapter's version. One page. Five parts: the model in one line; the
lineage in four steps (invented, proved it pays, copied for AI, everyone's model) with a "what we take
from it" column; who plays which role at Kaagaz; what we copy and what we do not; the dated figures
with their source type.

Rules the page enforces:

- **Every figure has a date and a source type.** "Palantir invented the role" is not a row.
  "First FDEs placed from 2005 (Perspective AI, May 2026, vendor)" is.
- **The "we do not copy" column has at least three rows.** A lineage map that only admires is a
  poster. Kaagaz is forty people in Hyderabad with a lender; six-month embeds, a customer-only CRM and
  hourly pricing are the obvious three.
- **The Echo row says who carries it.** At Kaagaz, you. Write it down so that the Echo's questions get
  calendar time.

Then compare with [`worksheet/godavari-lineage-map.md`](worksheet/godavari-lineage-map.md) row by row.
The differences are where your reading of the chapter and the chapter's reading of the sources
differ; most of them are worth a sentence in your notes for the interview.

## 9.3 Part B: the timeline, as data, checked

`data/fde-timeline.csv` holds nineteen rows, one per dated event in sections 05 and 06, with five
columns: `date` (YYYY, YYYY-MM or YYYY-MM-DD), `event`, `who`, `source`, `source_type` (one of
primary, company, vendor, recruiter, press, job board). `code/timeline.py` reads it, rejects any row
with a bad date, an empty source or an unknown type, sorts by date, writes the Markdown table and
prints the count per source type.

The real run:

```text
$ python -X utf8 code/timeline.py
19 rows, 2003 to 2026-10-04, written to fde-timeline.md
rows per source type:
  vendor     7
  company    7
  press      2
  primary    1
  recruiter  1
  job board  1
8 of 19 rows rest on vendor or recruiter content; quote them as such
```

It took 0.2 seconds. The last line is the reason the script exists. Eight of nineteen rows, and all of
the early Palantir dates, rest on a vendor blog that sells to FDE teams. The one primary source is a
former employee's essay. The company rows are companies describing themselves. That is the floor under
the story, and you should be able to say so in an interview without apology: the dates are approximate,
the shape is well attested.

The rendered table is in [`data/fde-timeline.md`](data/fde-timeline.md); section 06.1 is the
human-readable version with the adopter rows merged. Add a row whenever you read a dated claim about
the role; the script will refuse it until it has a source and a type.

What the script refuses, from a deliberately broken copy of the CSV (one row dated "circa 2014", one
row with its source deleted and its type set to "blog"):

```text
problems (fix the CSV before trusting the table):
  - line 4: date 'circa 2014' is not YYYY, YYYY-MM or YYYY-MM-DD
  - line 16: no source; a dated claim without a source is a rumour
  - line 16: source_type 'blog' not in ['company', 'job board', 'press', 'primary', 'recruiter', 'vendor']
```

Exit code 1, no table written. Exercise 2.4 asks you to extend the check.

## 9.4 Part C: the day-one note

This is the artifact the Part 1 plan asks for, and the one the CEO will actually read. The constraints:

- **One page.** The CEO is replying to an email, not reading a strategy.
- **Two columns.** A forward deployed engagement versus a services proposal, on the same rows: what
  Godavari buys, where the work happens, who decides what to build, what "done" means, what Kaagaz
  gets, what it costs, the risk.
- **No model names, no architecture.** You have not seen a loan file. The Day 7 anti-pattern is just
  as fatal in a note to your own CEO as in an interview.
- **Three asks.** Reply with a question not a number; do not quote a custom build; the first "will not
  build" line.
- **One paragraph of lineage.** Why this shape: Palantir's fifteen years, the AI-era copies, the
  published failure Kaagaz cannot afford. The lineage map carries the dates; the note carries the
  argument.

Write yours first. The chapter's is [`worksheet/godavari-day-one-note.md`](worksheet/godavari-day-one-note.md).
Day 3 will put numbers in the "what Kaagaz gets" and "what it costs" rows; Day 4 picks the problem the
note deliberately does not; Day 10 is the discovery document the note promises.

## 9.5 Part D: Godavari as a Delta would have described it

## 9.7 Build it with AI

An FDE in 2026 would use a chat or coding agent here in two ways: to pressure-test the lineage map and
the note, and to draft the script. The script is routine (an agent writes it correctly in one pass,
and the one thing to check is that it refuses rows rather than silently skipping them). The first use is
where the value and the danger are.

**Context to provide:** sections 02 to 07 of this chapter, or at least the timeline CSV and the
section 06.1 table; the Kaagaz facts (40 people, a document-and-workflow platform, no customer success
function); the Godavari facts from CURRICULUM.md; the rule that every figure must carry a date and a
source type; the rule that the day-one note names no technology.

**A strong prompt (pressure-test, not draft):**

> Here is my one-page lineage map and my day-one note for a lender engagement, and the timeline CSV
> they draw on. Do not rewrite them. Review them as a sceptical CEO and then as a hiring manager at an
> AI vendor would: (1) which figures have no date or no source type; (2) which claims about Palantir,
> OpenAI, Anthropic or Atlassian are not in the CSV, and for each say whether you know a source or are
> guessing; (3) where the "we do not copy" column is missing something a forty-person vendor could not
> afford; (4) whether the note names any technology; (5) whether the note's three asks would survive a
> head of sales who wants a price today. Cite the CSV row or the section for every point. Do not add
> any date, figure, company or quotation that is not in the material I gave you.

**What the agent typically gets right:** the structure of both pages; the two-column comparison; the
list of adopters; plausible interview questions; the script; spotting an undated figure when told to
look for one.

**What it typically gets wrong for this concept (check each):**

| Mistake | How it shows | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **invents dates and figures** | "Palantir created the FDE role in 2008"; "OpenAI has 120 FDEs"; "Atlassian's program started in 2024" | every figure must trace to a CSV row; the prompt forbids additions; anything without a row is deleted, not corrected from memory |
| **swaps the names** | Echo as the product engineer, Delta as the strategist, or both as "consultants" | section 02.1's mapping; the AI draft in `worksheet/ai-draft-lineage.md` does exactly this |
| **flattens the source types** | a vendor blog's 2005 and a first-hand 2015 account presented with the same confidence | the type column exists so this cannot happen silently; make the agent mark each |
| **describes the role as pre-sales or as consulting** | "the FDE builds the proof of concept that closes the deal"; "bills the customer for deployment hours" | section 07's edges; the loop; "owns the outcome, not the recommendation" |
| **puts a solution in the note** | "we would deploy a RAG pipeline over the loan documents in week one" | the note is technology-free by rule; grep it for model, RAG, vector, OCR, fine-tune |
| **omits the failure** | a lineage with no dark ages, no refusal, no "we do not copy" | ask it for the three ways the model fails and the fix for each; if it cannot, section 04 |
| **agrees with you** | "this is a strong lineage map" plus two cosmetic edits | give it the role of a head of sales who wants a quote today and ask it to argue for the services proposal; then answer the argument in the note |
| **misreads the India figure** | "postings grew 19 percent"; "average salary 1.2 crore" | nearly 19 times, June 2025 to June 2026, Indeed via Analytics India Magazine, October 2026; Glassdoor January 2026 about 12 lakh on 28 submissions |

The first and second are the ones to watch. An agent writing history produces confident, well-formed
dates and names that are wrong, and it does so in the same register as the ones that are right. The
timeline CSV with its source-type column is the defence: a claim either has a row or it is not in the
map.

## 9.8 How do you know it's right?

Whether a human or an agent wrote the artifacts, the checks are the same.

1. **Every figure in the map traces to a CSV row.** Read the map's dated-figures table against
   `data/fde-timeline.csv`. A figure without a row is deleted or gets a row with a source.
2. **The script runs clean and the source-type counts are honest.** If the vendor count went down
   because you relabelled a vendor blog as "company", that is the lie the script exists to prevent.
3. **The names are right.** Delta is the FDSE, Echo the deployment strategist, Dev the product
   engineer. Check against section 02.1, not against memory.
4. **The note names no technology.** `grep -i -n "llm\|rag\|vector\|model\|ocr\|fine-tune\|gpt\|claude"`
   over the note returns nothing. ("No model names" in the header is allowed; be honest about it.)
5. **The note's two columns are on the same rows.** If the services column has a row the FDE column
   lacks, the comparison is rigged.
6. **The "we do not copy" column has three rows and each one costs Kaagaz something.** "We do not copy
   Palantir's government customers" is not a row; "we do not run six-month embeds" is.
7. **The Echo row names a person.** "The team" is not a person.
8. **The reflective exercise is in the first person and names the operators' day.** If Lakshmi's
   tracker, the 6 pm batch or the fourteen documents are absent, it is a job description, not a
   Delta's account.
9. **The "why FDE?" sentence contains "outcome" and not "consulting", and could not be said by a
   solutions engineer.** Read it aloud to someone who has never heard of the role.
10. **Someone who was not you can refresh it next year.** Each figure's source is a thing they could
    fetch; the script would tell them what is missing.

**Review checklist:**

- [ ] lineage map: model in one line; four steps with "what we take from it"; roles at Kaagaz with the Echo carried by a named person; three or more "do not copy" rows; dated figures with source types
- [ ] every figure in the map has a CSV row; the script runs clean; source-type counts unchanged by relabelling
- [ ] names correct: Delta, Echo, Dev
- [ ] day-one note: one page; same rows in both columns; three asks; one lineage paragraph; no technology words
- [ ] the "will not build" line is written and is something Godavari would actually ask for
- [ ] the Delta exercise is first person, names the operators' day, and the translation table has six rows marked same, adapted or does not apply
- [ ] the "why FDE?" sentence passes the solutions-engineer test
- [ ] vendor and recruiter figures are labelled as such in the map and in anything you would say in an interview

## 9.9 Extend it

- Add a `claim_used_in` column to the CSV naming the section or artifact that quotes each row, and
  make the script warn about rows nothing uses (the timeline should not carry trivia).
- Add a `refresh_by` date to each row (a year from the source date) and print the rows past it. Part
  1's figures are meant to be refreshed yearly; the script can be the reminder.
- Write the lineage map for a different vendor: a vertical-AI company selling to hospitals, with
  Krishna District Hospital Network (Day 42) as the customer. Which "do not copy" rows change?
