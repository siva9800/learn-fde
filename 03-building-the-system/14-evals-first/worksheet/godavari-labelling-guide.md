## 1. What this is for

You are writing down what a careful Godavari analyst would have typed and decided for each file, so that the
system can be measured against your judgement and nobody else's. Label the file as you would process it on a
normal day, with the paper in front of you. Do not look at what the system extracted; you will not be shown it.

Who labels: Lakshmi (Vijayawada) and the senior analyst (Hyderabad) label every file independently. Neither
sees the other's label. Disagreements are settled by the rule in section 8.

## 2. The files

The golden set is 200 real files: 150 test files and 50 development files. You will not be told which is which.
They were chosen to over-represent the hard cases: phone photos, handwritten Telugu, files that were sent back,
and the two pilot branches, Kukatpally and Guntur. Most will not be clean. That is the point.

## 3. Documents and completeness

3.1 For each document on the product's checklist (fourteen for two-wheeler, the fourteenth only when a
co-applicant is named; nine for gold), mark present or absent. Present means the document is in the file and is
the document it says it is. A photocopy of the reverse side alone is not the document.

3.2 The file is incomplete if any checklist document is absent. You do not decide whether an absent document
matters; the checklist does. The compliance head signs the completeness labels.

3.3 Mark the page number where you found each document.

## 4. Fields

Type each mandatory field in the box for it. Twelve fields per product (the list is on the form).

4.1 **Address**: the full address as on the application form, including the city and the PIN code.

4.2 **PAN**: only from the PAN card. If there is no PAN card in the file, leave PAN blank, even if the form shows
a PAN. The file goes back for the card; the form's PAN is the customer's claim, not the evidence.

4.3 **Applicant name**: spelled as on the PAN card. A transliteration variant on a handwritten form
("RAAVI" for "RAVI") is not the name.

4.4 **Amounts**: rupees, as written. "Rs 1,25,000" and "125000" are the same to the scorer; type whichever you
type at work. Never convert: if a declaration says a figure without saying monthly or annual, type it as written
and add a note.

4.5 **Dates**: day, month, year, as on the document. Slashes or dashes do not matter.

4.6 **Cannot read it**: type UNREADABLE. Do not guess. The item goes to the team leads, who read the page with you.

4.7 **Not in the file**: if the document a field comes from is absent, leave the field blank.

Format is forgiven by the registered normaliser (case, spacing, Rs and commas, slashes, a space in a mobile
number). Content is never forgiven: one wrong character is a wrong value.

## 5. Would you send it back?

Answer yes or no, and tick every reason that applies, from the list on the form: a missing checklist
document; address proof not on the signed list; address proof not in the applicant's own name; utility bill
older than 60 days; Aadhaar not masked; bank statement stale; amount above 90 percent of the on-road price;
amount above the gold LTV cap. A reason that is not on the list (for example "photograph unclear") may be
written in the notes box; the compliance head decides whether it is a send-back reason.

## 6. Your steps (the trajectory)

The form records the order in which you open pages and check things, with the page number each time, and how
long the file took. Work as you normally would. These steps are the benchmark for any automated workflow
later (Day 17), and the timings are the human cost the business case uses.

## 7. Time and pace

About 40 to 50 minutes a file. Book no more than three hours of labelling in one day, never in the last three
days of a month, through your team lead (Day 8). If you are tired, stop: a tired label is a wrong label.

## 8. Adjudication

1. Both labels are normalised. If they are then equal, that is the label.
2. A field typed differently goes to Srinivas and the Vijayawada team lead, who read the source page together
   and record what is on the paper, citing this guide.
3. A document, completeness or send-back disagreement goes to the compliance head, who decides against the
   signed rules and the alternatives list. If no signed rule covers it, the file is sent back and the case
   goes on the rule-gap list.
4. Nobody from Kaagaz adjudicates. An agreed label is final unless the double-keyed audit or a later finding
   reopens it; reopening creates a new version of the truth file, never an edit to the old one.

## 9. Personal data

The files are real customers' files. They stay on the machine in the Hyderabad building (decision D08). Do not
copy, photograph or print them. Labels are stored with the file number only. Kaagaz receives scores and masked
examples, never the values.
