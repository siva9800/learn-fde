# Transcript 06 · The core-system owner

> Day 6 discovery pack · Interview 6 of 10 · Persona: admin (system owner) · Where: the Hyderabad data centre's meeting room · When: week 1, Thursday, 11:00 · Length: 60 minutes · Recording: none · Synthetic conversation for the course.

**In the room:** the core-system owner (O), one engineer who has run the 2009 core lending system for fourteen years; the FDE (F).

## Conversation

**F:** I have read the WSDL. Before I ask about it, I want to hear how a file moves through your system in a day, in your words.

**O:** Branch scanners drop PDFs into the branch's SFTP folder during the day. At two in the morning a job moves them to the region folders and creates a shell application record with the branch code and the file number. Analysts fill the record in the morning. At six in the evening the credit-check batch runs against the bureau and the internal rules, for every record that was completed since the previous six. Then the memo, then sanction. The batch is the day. If you miss six, you wait until tomorrow's six.

**F:** Can the batch run more often?

**O:** It could run at noon as well. It has not, because the bureau contract is priced per batch and because the last time somebody changed the batch schedule the month-end run overran into the morning and the branches could not log in. That was 2021. I was here until four.

**F:** What time does the batch finish?

**O:** Normally by eight. Month end, eleven. Quarter end, I stay.

**F:** The SOAP API. Fourteen operations; CreateApplication has sixty-one fields, twenty-three mandatory. What does it do if I call it twice with the same file?

**O:** It creates two applications. There is no key on your side. The record ID is generated by us. The last vendor, in 2022, had a timeout on their side, retried, and we had four hundred duplicate applications by the time anyone noticed. I cleaned them by hand over a weekend.

**F:** So if we write to it, the idempotency is ours to build. We would derive a key from the file number and the branch code and check for an existing record before every create.

**O:** Check with GetApplicationByFileNumber. It exists. Nobody uses it.

**F:** Is there a sandbox?

**O:** A copy of production refreshed every quarter, masked. The last refresh was in July. Rules in the sandbox are three months behind. Do not trust the credit-check results in it, trust the field validation.

**F:** Who decides whether we can write to production?

**O:** The CISO decides whether you may connect. I decide whether you may write. I will say yes when I have seen your calls in the sandbox for a month, every one logged, with no duplicates and nothing outside the batch window.

**F:** What is the batch window?

**O:** Nothing writes between five thirty and eight in the evening. Reads are fine. Writes during the batch have corrupted the sequence twice.

**F:** How does the system know which branch a user may see?

**O:** From AD at login. The user's branch group maps to a branch code; the regional group to a region. Every query carries the branch filter. It is not optional and it is not in the API; it is in the session. If you call the API with a service account, you get what the service account's groups allow, which today is nothing, because it has none.

**F:** What is your bad day?

**O:** Quarter end. Everyone's files, the batch overrunning, and somebody from a vendor on the phone asking why their call timed out.

**F:** Three vendors have failed on this system. What did they have in common?

**O:** They sent me a design in week eight. By week eight I had already decided.

**F:** Then here is what I will do instead. This week: one record in the sandbox, with our key, with your GetApplication check, logged. Next week I bring the log and you tell me what is wrong with it.

**O:** Bring the log.

## Read-back

Confirmed: two batches (02:00 transfer, 18:00 credit check), the write window, no idempotency and the lookup operation, the quarterly sandbox with stale rules, his condition for production writes, the branch filter in the session.
