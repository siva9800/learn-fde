# Transcript 10 · Bhavani, junior analyst, Vijayawada

> Day 6 discovery pack · Interview 10 of 10 · Persona: operator (junior) · Where: her desk, Vijayawada credit-ops floor · When: week 2, Thursday, 10:00, on a second visit, after shadowing her for one full file · Length: 35 minutes · Recording: none · Synthetic conversation for the course.

**In the room:** Bhavani (BH), two years as an analyst, the newest person on the Vijayawada floor; the FDE (F).

## Conversation

**F:** I watched you do one file this morning. I want to go through it again, but this time you tell me what you were thinking at each step. You opened the tracker first.

**BH:** Madam's row for me. Lakshmi assigns the juniors' files now. I check the colour. Green is new. Then I open the PDF and the core. The core takes forever; everybody says forty seconds but it feels longer.

**F:** How did you learn the steps? From the SOP?

**BH:** I was given the SOP on my first day. It has twelve steps. I read it once. I learned the job from the tracker and from sitting beside Lakshmi for a month. The tracker's columns are the steps. "Received", "keyed", "docs checked", "waiting on", "verified", "batch", "memo". Seven columns and three colours.

**F:** When you are not sure about something, what do you do?

**BH:** Ask Lakshmi. For the address proof question especially. Is a rent agreement enough? Is the gas bill in the father's name okay? I send the file back if she is busy, because sending back is safe. Nobody gets an audit query for sending a file back.

**F:** So a send-back is sometimes a junior being careful, not a document being missing.

**BH:** Yes. Maybe a third of mine.

**F:** Show me the last page that came to you on WhatsApp.

**BH:** (shows the phone) A passbook page, from Guntur, yesterday. I saved it, printed it, stapled it. In the tracker I wrote "passbook received WA". The core file does not have it.

**F:** Where are the branch numbers you call?

**BH:** In my phone. Lakshmi gave me her list. The list in the core is old.

**F:** When you make a mistake, who catches it?

**BH:** Lakshmi reviews the juniors' files before the batch. She catches most. The credit manager catches some. I have had one audit query in two years and it was for a photo that was in the paper file but not scanned.

**F:** What is the slowest part of your day?

**BH:** Typing the sixty fields with the screen loading between every tab. And then the phone calls. I spend the afternoon on the phone to branches for files I keyed in the morning.

**F:** What is a bad day?

**BH:** The day after a holiday. Two batches' worth of files and the branches all phoning to ask where theirs is.

**F:** If a system typed the fields and checked the fourteen documents before the file reached you, what would you want to see?

**BH:** A list of what is wrong with the file, and the rule. So that I can send it back with a reason the branch understands, instead of "KYC pending". And so that I do not have to ask Lakshmi whether a gas bill is okay. If the rule says yes, it is yes.

**F:** And if the system read a figure wrongly?

**BH:** I would not know. That is the honest answer. I do not read the slip against the figure the way Lakshmi does; I do not have time. If it is wrong, it goes through until she or the credit manager catches it.

## Read-back

Confirmed: the tracker as the way she learned the job, the seven columns and three colours, send-backs as caution, WhatsApp pages from Guntur, the phone list, Lakshmi's review as the error check, the day after a holiday as the bad day, and her honest answer that she would not catch a wrongly read figure.
