## 1.1 Friday of week four

It is Friday, 20 November 2026, at Godavari Finance's Hyderabad office. Around the table: the COO,
the CISO, the compliance head, Srinivas and the Vijayawada team lead, Lakshmi and the senior analyst
from Hyderabad, and the core-system owner, who came because his conditions are on page eleven.
Kaagaz's engagement lead is on your side of the table. This is the week-4 checkpoint from Day 8's
plan, and it has one question: "Do you commit to a two-branch pilot in December, on these terms and
these numbers?"

You have spent four weeks on that question. Day 4 picked the problem. Day 5 drew the map. Day 6 found
nineteen steps where the SOP had twelve. Day 7 broke the goal into six workstreams and a skeleton.
Day 8 dated it. Day 9 measured the baseline and found that the honest business case is thin. Six
artifacts, about fourteen thousand words, in six folders.

Nobody in the room will read six folders. They will read one document, and some of them will read
only one page of it. If that document is a report ("here is everything we learned"), the meeting
becomes a discussion of what you learned, and it ends with "let us take this offline". If it is a
**decision document** ("here is what we propose, here is why, here is what you are signing"), the
meeting ends with signatures, four written changes and a pilot on 7 December.

This chapter is about the second kind: how to assemble it from the artifacts you already have, write
it for three readers at once, run the meeting where it is signed, and say no in writing to the
requests that would sink the engagement.

## 1.2 Why it matters for your career

Three reasons, from the research behind the course (CURRICULUM.md and the Part 2 plan; sources in
section 13):

- **Interviewers ask you to describe this artifact.** "Walk me through the document you wrote at the
  end of discovery" is a standard hiring-manager question, because the answer shows whether you
  scoped before building, who you thought mattered, and what you refused.
- **The client-simulation round tests the readout.** The Harvard FAS 2026 guide (fetched October
  2026) describes a 45-minute round where the interviewer plays a customer, "sometimes friendly,
  sometimes deliberately frustrated", and the tasks include presenting a solution, defending a
  trade-off and pushing back "without losing the relationship". That is the readout, compressed.
- **Customer-facing discovery is the fastest-rising bucket.** It appears in 70 percent or more of the
  1,000 FDE postings analysed in 2026 (Day 2). The discovery document is the visible output of that
  bucket: the first time the customer sees you as a peer who has understood their business, not a
  vendor with a demo.

On the job the stakes are simpler. An engagement without a signed discovery document has no agreed
problem, no agreed numbers and no agreed refusals. Every week after that, someone reopens one of the
three.

> **Analogy (Telugu audience).** The village panchayat. You lay out the case before the elders. Every
> elder may object, and each objection is heard in front of everyone. The decision is made with all of
> them present and written into the register, so that nobody can reopen it next month by saying "I
> never agreed to that". The discovery document is the register; the readout is the panchayat.

## 1.4 The hard parts, named up front

1. **Assembly feels like copying.** It is not. The skill is choosing what each reader needs from
   fourteen thousand words and cutting the rest to a link. Most first drafts are three times too long.
2. **The room includes people who can veto.** The CISO and the compliance head did not come to be
   persuaded by enthusiasm. They came to see whether their conditions are in writing. Section 03
   writes for them; section 04 runs the meeting with them.
3. **Saying no to the sponsor, in front of his peers.** The COO will ask for the chatbot. You will say
   "not now" to the person who pays, with the CISO watching. Section 05 gives you the sentence and the
   alternative that makes it land.
4. **The document will change.** Four changes in the room on Friday, more at week 6. A document that is
   rewritten every time loses its history; section 06 versions it instead.
